WHAT TO BUILD
A small software 3D renderer in pure Java that draws through the game's 2D `Gfx` interface (convex polygons with `fillPoly`, plus the other primitives), and with it the town as a low-poly 3D world seen from a LOW perspective camera. No libraries and no OpenGL: it must run in the desktop harness (Java2D `AwtGfx`) so we can take screenshots and test, and through the Canvas-backed `AndroidGfx` on the phone.

Requirements
1. Camera: perspective. Three presets on one engine, smoothly interpolated. STREET (the default): behind and above the player, pitch about 18-25 degrees below the horizon, looking along the street so buildings rise and overlap and there is sky and a horizon. WALK: follows the walking character with damping and a slight lead in the direction of travel. OVERVIEW: rises to about 50-55 degrees so the whole town can be seen and a destination chosen; then it dives back down when the walk starts. In STREET a horizontal drag orbits the view and it eases back.
2. World: the same layout as `City` (City.B[i] x, y, w, h and door positions, ROADS_H / ROADS_V, `City.path`) so walking distances and gameplay do not change: the 3D scene is a view of the same town. Add ground (asphalt with lane marks and crossings, pavements, grass, dirt), the nine key buildings as distinct low-poly models with readable silhouettes and identity (take the designs from the illustrator's `Icons.building`, branch worktree-agent-a46ae1660320ab02d, picture scratchpad/art-icons/buildings-140.png; the environment artist's top-down city in scratchpad/art-city and its code, when its branch lands, may give you props and palette), filler buildings and a far skyline so the town feels like one corner of a big city, trees, street lamps, parked and moving cars, pedestrians, and small life: laundry, bakery smoke, neon flicker, the alley cat, birds.
3. People: the player and pedestrians as upright billboards scaled by depth with ground shadows. Try `PersonArt.draw(Gfx c, float x, float y, float s, Look look, boolean female, float phase, boolean walking, int facing)` from branch worktree-agent-a7014298bd5b646aa (copy nothing into your branch: if you need it to compile, read it with `git show` and write your figures against a tiny interface, or draw simple figures yourself; say which you did). The player must read clearly at phone size.
4. Light and time: the minute of the day drives sun direction and colour (flat shading per face from a light vector plus ambient), the sky gradient, long shadows at dusk, night with lit windows, lamp pools and neon, and aerial perspective (distance tint toward the horizon colour). Nothing flashes faster than 3 Hz.
5. Labels: building names are drawn with `c.text` at projected positions as small sign chips that face the camera (no text baked into geometry). Open or closed must be visible without relying on colour alone.
6. Picking: `int pick(float sx, float sy)` returns the building id under a touch: projected silhouette bounds, nearest first, never smaller than 48x48 units; the sign chip is a target too.
7. Correctness: near-plane clipping of polygons (a low camera puts geometry behind the eye), back-face culling, frustum culling, painter's ordering that does not pop while the camera moves (sort convex parts by depth, split what must be split), no cracks between ground tiles, no NaN anywhere.
8. Performance: zero allocation per frame (preallocated arrays, in-place sort; `fillPoly` takes an exact-length array, so keep one reusable array per vertex count), at most about 2,500 polygons per frame in STREET, and print polygon counts and desktop milliseconds per frame from your bench. Keep the projected output in flat arrays so a later batched triangle call in `Gfx` could replace per-polygon calls.
9. Files (new only, package com.wizzcraft.ima.core): for example `Cam3` (math and camera), `Scene3` or `Mesh3` (geometry, lighting, sorting, drawing), `City3` (the town: build once; `update(dt)`; `draw(Gfx c, float top, float bottom)`; `pick`; camera control; setters for the player's position in City units, heading and walking flag, the minute, a destination highlight, open flags), and a desktop bench `tools/desktop/com/wizzcraft/ima/core/City3Shots.java` that writes screenshots and prints `CITY3 OK` after checks (no NaN, polygon budget, no allocation growth across 600 frames, `pick` returns each building from its sign position in OVERVIEW). Look at the existing desktop tools (MiniLab, AwtGfx, CountGfx) for the pattern. Do not edit MapScreen, City, Game or any existing file: integration is done later from your notes.
