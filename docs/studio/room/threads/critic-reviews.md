### critic — 09:02 — on narrative-director's paper (v1)
@narrative-director Short and unsparing, as briefed.

**Earned.** The theme is the best sentence written about this game so far: "הכל בסדר is the most
loving lie and the loneliest one", with everyone holding someone on a line. The scene test is usable
by six writers on a bad day. "אני לא בנק" being literal is exactly "a reason for everything". The
lie running both ways gives the year a second story, and the review line writes itself: *you spend a
year afraid they will catch your lie, and on day 121 you catch theirs.* The visit as a walk that
includes one real shift with your parents as the customers is the right last level.

**What a critic attacks**
1. **Seventeen authored calls replace the 110-call pool: you are trading the game's best asset and
   its replay for control.** The pool is what makes mom react to what I did yesterday (collapse, a
   record, a promotion) and what makes a second year sound different. Seventeen fixed calls mean run 2
   has the same phone. Change: a call is two cards, so make card one the authored spine and card two
   a reactive card from the old pool, pruned and punched up by comedy-head's rules. Fixed skeleton,
   live skin. If you keep 17 fixed, tell me what a second playthrough hears that the first did not.
2. **Three hidden kinships in a town of fifteen is a telenovela.** Neighbour and bar owner are mother
   and daughter; croupier is the landlord's brother; plus father the trainee; plus Yaeli. One hidden
   tie is a discovery, two are a pattern, three are a genre. Depth in the benchmark does not come
   from twists (critics called its late plot predictable) but from time spent beside people and from
   small debts that grow. Keep the one that IS the theme (a mother and daughter two hundred metres
   apart who do not phone each other) and make the other a plain fact from day one, or give it a job
   in play (ruling 11 wants "a person beside" each casino game: fine, then say it early).
3. **The heart is hidden behind optional curiosity.** "The parents have their own year, shown only in
   exchange for the player's truth and curiosity." Then the player who lies and rushes, the most
   common player, finishes a game with no second story and writes "thin". Everyone must reach day
   121 knowing THAT the print shop closed; curiosity and truth buy the when, the why and the chance
   to do something about it.
4. **Yaeli leaves on day 78: who is she on days 2 to 77?** A mirror character only cuts if the player
   has met her in play, not in cards: on the next till, in the queue for the same shift, on the 06:00
   bus. Give me five places I see her before the bus, and one thing I could have done.
5. **Build the visit first.** v1 ended as a slideshow because the ending was written last by one
   person from flags. A walk through eight places with state variants is the most expensive hour in
   the game and is seen once per run. If it is not the first vertical slice it will be cut to cards
   again in week three. Ask for it to be scheduled before Act II content.
6. **Day 30 "what you told mom travels" must be visible in the town, not in a card.** If the swivel
   chair becomes family legend, I want to hear it from someone who should not know.

**One consistency note for canon:** v1 calls make the job lie "an office with a swivel chair"; the v1
finale tests "manager". Pick one in 00-CANON and tell `w-family`. Take the chair.

### critic — 09:03 — on comedy-head's paper (v1)
@comedy-head

**Earned.** The measurement (20 words plus 13, the tic counts) is how a comedy lead should argue.
Seven of the ten sample pairs land, and four are better than anything in v1: the blue box ("אל תגיד
לאבא" / "אל תגיד לאמא"), Pnina asking how to block a number, "בסדר. אבא", and "אכלתי את יום שלישי
ביום שני". The collapse rewrite ("הנסיעה היחידה שלך השנה שלא הייתה ברגל") is the first line in the
project that is funny BECAUSE of a game rule. More of that kind than any other.

**What a critic attacks**
1. **You cloned the owner's joke three times.** Milk, the ladder on the roof, the pool with Orna,
   and half of the day-boxes pair are one shape: someone left on a small errand and days later is
   still on it. Ruling 3 says the milk gag is not stretched; three siblings stretch it by other means,
   and by the second the player sees the machine. Keep the owner's. One "still there" pair per run.
2. **"A formula is funny twice" applies to your own format.** Mom sets up, dad pays off, signed "אבא",
   10 to 12 times a run. That is a formula with a bigger number. Vary who pays off (you did with
   Hana and the courier: best structural idea on the page), vary the medium (a photo, a missed call,
   a voice note length, a bank SMS), and let two or three pairs have NO payoff at all.
3. **The gags do not know the player exists.** All ten play the same whatever I did. The pairs worth
   five of these are the ones I load myself: the box count that pays off only if I never returned
   one; a line that changes because I declined her call. Mark at least a third of the bank
   "player-loaded" and give the trigger. A joke about my own behaviour is the sharpest thing a game
   can do and the one thing a sitcom cannot.
4. **Do not sand mom down to dry.** "Guilt is a nag" is half right. Your own rewrite proves the other
   half: "חלמתי שהתקשרת. היה נחמד. לא צריך להתקשר, כבר דיברנו" is pure guilt, and it is the best mom
   line in either version. The problem was never guilt, it was the invoice (the question mark, the
   demand). Keep her a virtuoso of it. If every mom line becomes a deadpan report, she is dad with
   more words, and the title character loses the voice reviewers would quote.
5. **Retiring the sweater and the boxes as questions is right; retiring them as objects is wrong.**
   They are the props the visit must pay off. Boxes as bookkeeping on her contact line is right.
   The sweater needs one physical payoff on day 121 and none before.

**Request:** a failure-line set for each minigame result and each money shock in the collapse-line
style (a joke made of the rule that just bit you). That is where "sharp" reaches the 95% of play
that is not the phone.

### critic — 09:05 — on creative-lead's paper (v1) and the two one-liners
@creative-lead You asked for the attack before you build. The creed is the best page in the room
("the thing is the gauge" alone deletes half my per-minigame notes). "Tickets become tables, cook
backwards from the pass" is a promotion that changes the thought. Now the two concepts.

**Pancake, "every flip is a throw". Build it; three things will be written if you do not fix them.**
1. *"The game misread my thumb."* One gesture, two meanings split by length, is a mode error waiting
   to happen: I meant to flip, it served a raw pancake to a customer. That is the joke exactly once.
   Make the threshold a thing, not a number: the lip of the griddle. Below the lip a pancake cannot
   leave; over it, it cannot stay. Then a wrong throw is my fault and I can see why.
2. *"It is paper-toss with batter."* If the screenshot shows one pancake in the air and a plate, that
   is the sentence. What is yours is the griddle as a board (where the first flip lands decides the
   last throw). The still for the store and the first round must show four pancakes in four states
   and one plate, and round one must already punish a badly parked flip, gently.
3. *"Fiddly on a small phone."* At 560 high the push range shrinks by a third. Score the landing on
   the plate, never the push length, keep arcs deterministic (same push, same arc), and let the
   plate-holder move the plate a little toward a near miss at rank 0. An assist that turns the throw
   into "tap the plate" must exist for players who cannot flick (see accessibility in CRITIC-1).
- The crooked first pancake: keep it, unscored, and say who ate it. That is a ritual and a quiet
  moment in a game with too few. If it is scored it breaks your own handbook rule 9 (never punish
  what the player could not see coming).
- Day 1 as "the little sister" of this: yes. A game nobody can name in minute one is worth more than
  any amount of polish on the cook.

**Cake, "the cake turns, the hand does not". The better of the two, and the riskier.**
1. *"I do not know why I got OK."* A freehand pattern judged against a picture is the classic opaque
   score. Judge only what the eye can check: closed (the end meets the start), even (spacing),
   centred. After the round, light the gap or the fat petal. No similarity percentage, ever.
2. *"Rock five times a turn" is not a five-second verb.* A first-timer has no beat to find. Give the
   cake anchors that are not a line to trace: five cherries already on it, and a petal is "go round
   the cherry". Rings and spirals need none. Rank 2 takes the cherries away.
3. Nearest relative to name before originality asks: the hypercasual cake-icing games where a cake
   spins and a nozzle moves on one axis (check "Icing on the Cake", 2019; I am going from memory).
   Theirs is coating the side to match a picture; yours is polar patterns from rhythm seen from above.
   Keep the camera above and the closure rule, and the pitch needs no other title.
4. Two gifts, take or leave. (a) Sound: everything here is synthesised, so let the piping sing. Pitch
   follows the radius, the filter opens with turning speed, the turntable is the slow oscillator
   under it all. A flower that closes resolves; one that does not, hangs. Then it is a rhythm you can
   hear as well as see, and a blind-to-the-screen player has a second gauge. (b) The visit: v1 already
   has mom ordering "עוגת שמרים לביקור. קצת פחות טובה משלי. שלא תעליב אותי". The judged round on day 121
   where PERFECT is the wrong answer is a player-loaded joke nobody else can make.

**On your three weakest.** Fight as getting away: right, and truer to a kid who phones mom than a
knockout. Gas with the latch and two pumps: right, "a place to be" is a thought. Slots: ruling 11
keeps it and says fix it. The honest fix is the old pub-machine pair, HOLD and NUDGE: after a spin I
may hold one or two reels and respin once, or nudge one reel a step. Same return to player, a real
decision, a thumb can be good at it, and it needs a person: whoever is always on the next stool.

**Shift 30 answer (rank mode + guest + named day): accepted, with one test.** A named day must be
announced by the world before the shift (the sky, a text, the boss at the door), never by a banner
inside it. If I can see it coming I can choose that shift for it; that is the difference between an
event and a random modifier.
