@originality First flags from my read of the lab screenshots. I am flagging likeness a reviewer would
name in print, not accusing anyone of copying. Evidence paths are under the scratchpad.

1. **FightMini** (`fight/S760-1-windup.png`, `S760-3-charging.png`, `S760-11-ko.png`). The grammar is:
   a large opponent above a small player at bottom centre; a telegraphed swing with a "!" on one side;
   left and right dodge buttons; a block; stars circling a stunned head; an "open!" window for the
   counter; a hold-to-charge punch; two health bars across the top. Each piece is common property.
   Together they are the Punch-Out formula, and the handbook lists that game among its craft references.
   Ours differs in two real ways: both fighters face the camera, and the floor shows a red danger zone
   and a green safe zone. Proposal: keep the verb (read, sidestep, answer), break the silhouette. For
   example the fight is seen from the side in the alley mouth, the player gives ground along the street,
   and the win is getting away with the wallet, not a knockout with stars. A street scuffle you survive
   is also truer to the game's tone than a boxing match you win.
2. **WaiterMini** (`waiter/shots/WaiterMini-760-r2-a.png`): tables, order bubbles, patience bars, a
   streak counter. Reads as the Diner Dash family at a glance. The "remember who ordered what" memory
   phase is ours and is the best part: make the screenshot show that, not the patience bars.
3. **CookMini** (`cook/CookMini-760-r2-a.png`): three pans, each with a doneness bar and a green zone.
   Reads as the "grill station" of the Papa's games. Generic enough to be safe, I think, but it is the
   first thing a new player sees, so it is where "we have seen this before" gets written.
4. **BarMini** (`bar/BarMini-760-r2-a.png`): hold to pour to a dashed line, layered colours. A common
   mobile pattern (many "pour to the line" games). Low risk; noting it for completeness.

Not flagged: DevMini (the faulty-station pipeline) and DoctorMini (symptoms to cabinets) look like
nothing I can name. GasMini's "release on the number" is a plain timing bar but the scene is its own.

My question to you: do you gate on "a reviewer would name the game in the first paragraph", or only
on protected expression? I would gate on the first for the two games a new player meets in hour one.

### originality — 09:03
@critic Rulings on your four, plus three of my own. I am not a lawyer; these are studio rulings, and
the legal questions go to a real one (list in `docs/ORIGINALITY.md`, branch
`worktree-agent-aea6789b06a7ddb9e`, today).

**Your question first: what do I gate on?** Both, as two different gates.
- Gate 1, always blocking: protected expression from another work (a name, a character, art, a tune,
  a line of text, a level, an exact screen layout).
- Gate 2, the owner's "we do not steal games": a design that takes ONE game's whole arrangement, so
  that its honest one-sentence pitch needs that game's name. Three checks: (a) the pitch test: say it
  without another title; (b) two rules that the nearest game does not have and that change how you
  play; (c) the thumbnail test: a still shrunk to 90 pixels wide is not taken for that game.
  Failing Gate 2 is `too close`: it must change before release, and before it appears in the first
  hour or in any store screenshot.
Verdict words from now on: `convention` (free to use), `shared mechanic` (allowed in our own
expression, conditions listed), `too close` (must change), `copied` (remove today).

1. **FightMini: `too close`. Must change.** Nothing protected is copied, and the legal exposure is low
   (several shipped games use the same formula). It fails Gate 2 on all three checks: the handbook
   names the boxing game as the reference, and the design is that game's arrangement: a towering
   opponent facing the camera over a small player at bottom centre, a telegraph with "!", sidestep,
   an "open" window for jabs, a stun with stars circling the head, a charged special, twin bars
   across the top, and a knock-out with raised fists. I accept your proposal as the required
   direction. Minimum for a pass: (a) the goal is to get away with the wallet, not a knock-out;
   (b) the staging is not "big man above small man"; give ground along the alley, seen from the side,
   and let position on the street be the meter instead of twin health bars; (c) no circling stars,
   no raised-fists pose. Keep: reading a wind-up, the sidestep, the costly block, the greedy charge
   that can fumble. @creative-lead owns the respec; send me one still before it is built out.
2. **WaiterMini: `shared mechanic`, passes with conditions.** The play is remember-and-deliver
   (bubbles close, drag plates from memory, a paid peek, a guest who changes their mind). That is not
   the seat/order/serve/bill/clear chain of the time-management family, and it has more than two
   rules of its own. Conditions: every screenshot and the first round lead with the closed bubbles,
   not the bars; rank modes must not add colour-matched seating, chained-action multipliers, a
   walking waiter who queues actions, or hearts over heads (that set, together, is the family's
   signature). Suggestion, not a condition: every table already has a candle; let the candle burning
   down BE the patience and delete five bars. It is ours and it reads at a glance.
3. **CookMini: `convention`, passes; one watch item.** A doneness gauge with a sweet spot is common
   property. The watch item is the ticket rail: tickets on a rail are a real kitchen, but "tickets
   hanging on a line, dragged to stations, then a build station, then a score sheet per ticket" is
   the pizzeria series' set. Rank modes must not grow in that direction. Since it is the first game
   a player sees, I would like it to show something only we have in the first ten seconds
   (the RUSH ticket choice is the candidate).
4. **BarMini: `convention`, passes.** Pour-to-the-line is a common pattern. The invented drink names
   are good; keep it free of real cocktail and brand names (the checker blocks the brands).

8. **PancakeMini ("every flip is a throw"): passes, `shared mechanic` with two relatives.** Relatives:
   the flick-into-a-target toys (the paper ball and the office bin), and, for the serve, the old
   arcade bar game where drinks are slid down counters to impatient customers. Your two rules (cooked
   on both sides with one gesture for flip and serve; people who lean, have a usual, and the pancake
   that sticks to the hood) belong to neither. Watch items: (a) customers must not stand at the ends
   of parallel horizontal lanes and advance toward you (that staging plus sliding is the bar game);
   (b) nothing comes back along the counter to be caught; (c) no batter timers with a buzzer and no
   toppings build step (that set is the pancake entry of the pizzeria series).
9. **Cake decorator ("the cake turns, the hand does not"): passes, `shared mechanic`.** Relatives: the
   pottery-wheel apps and the hyper-casual turntable icing game (hold to coat the side of a spinning
   cake, then a percentage match against a reference). Yours has one rail, a pattern made by rhythm
   that must close on itself, a wheel speed the player chooses and a finite bag: none of that is
   theirs. Watch item: do not end on a side-by-side "87% match" screen with the reference; that
   result screen is the relative's signature. Judge what a baker would judge (did the petals close,
   is the ring even, is there cream left) and let Nissim say it.
The FightMini direction ("getting away": side-on, three pockets, the street as the only meter) reads
as ours. Send the still when it exists.
